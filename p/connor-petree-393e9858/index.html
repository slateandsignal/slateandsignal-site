Direction

Niche Directions
Direction 01
Developmental nutrition for babies and toddlers
This would center your content around how early nutrition directly affects developmental milestones. You'd create recipes specifically designed to support motor skills, cognitive development, and physical growth, explaining the why behind each ingredient choice. Your audience would be parents who are worried about delays and want to give their kids the best start, looking for someone who understands the science and can translate it into actual meals.
Direction 02
Practical first foods that actually work
This angle focuses on recipes that solve the real problems parents face when starting solids. You'd build around what actually gets eaten, what's easy to prepare, and what supports proper development without requiring a culinary degree or three hours of prep time. Your audience would be busy parents who want to do right by their kids but need recipes that fit into real life, and they'd follow you because your food actually works in practice.
Direction 03
Nutrition for thriving kids
This direction positions you as the go-to resource for parents who want their children to thrive, with recipes and guidance that go beyond just avoiding problems. You'd focus on what optimal nutrition looks like from the start, building content around foods that support energy, growth, and overall wellness. Your audience would be parents who are proactive about their kids' health and want clear, practical guidance they can trust.
Content Format
Talking-head videos with recipe demos
Based on your instinct to talk things through and your vision of selling your work in video format, your best fit is likely short to mid-length videos where you explain the why behind each recipe while showing how to make it. You could film yourself talking through the nutritional reasoning, then cut to hands-only shots of the actual prep and plating. This format lets you teach and connect at the same time, which matches how you communicate best. You'd post these on Instagram Reels, TikTok, or YouTube Shorts to build an audience, then monetize through a paid recipe library, a membership with exclusive content, or one-on-one consultations for parents dealing with specific feeding challenges.
A 60-second video where you explain why you always include a healthy fat with every meal for babies under two, then show three quick ways to add it (avocado smashed into oatmeal, a drizzle of olive oil on steamed veggies, a small scoop of nut butter stirred into yogurt). You talk directly to camera for the explanation, then cut to close-up hands-only shots of you actually doing each one. The hook is the why (brain development needs fat), the value is the how (three easy, specific ways to do it today).

06
Signal
Blind Spot
You assume people already understand why nutrition matters for development

Your answers show that you've done the work to understand how early nutrition affects development. You've learned how to help a child develop properly, and that knowledge opened your eyes to how little good information is out there. You see the problem clearly: the rise in developmental delays, the bad advice to feed babies slop. That's all real.

The gap is that most of your audience hasn't done that same learning yet. They might sense that something is wrong with the standard advice, or they might just be looking for easy recipes without understanding the stakes. Either way, they don't yet know what you know about the connection between nutrition and development. If you build content assuming they already get it, you'll lose them.

This means you need to teach the why before you can sell the what. As the reason the recipe matters. Every piece of advice needs to connect back to a specific outcome parents can see (better sleep, hitting milestones on time, fewer tantrums, sustained energy). Make the connection explicit every time.

Your content will work when parents understand that what they feed their baby in the first two years actually shapes how their brain and body develop, and that your recipes are built around that fact. That's the bridge you need to build in every post. Don't assume they already crossed it.

07
First
Move
Start posting recipes with the why built in

Your first move is to start creating and posting recipe content where every single post includes both the how and the why. Pick one recipe you already make, something simple that you know works for babies or toddlers, and film yourself talking through why it's built the way it is. Explain what each ingredient does for development, why the texture matters at this stage, or how it solves a common feeding problem. Then show how to make it.

Post that video on Instagram Reels or TikTok. Do it again with a different recipe in a few days. Do it again after that. Your goal right now is to build a small body of work that shows people what you know and how you teach. You're proving to yourself that you can create this content consistently and that people respond to it.

As you post, pay attention to which recipes get the most engagement and which explanations seem to land. You'll start to see patterns in what your audience actually needs, and that will tell you where to focus next. You might find that parents are desperate for toddler breakfast ideas, or that they're confused about when to introduce certain textures, or that they want more help with picky eating. Let the response guide your next content.

Once you have 15 to 20 posts up and you're seeing consistent engagement, you can start thinking about monetization. That might look like a paid recipe library, a membership with weekly meal plans, or one-on-one consultations for parents dealing with specific challenges. But you need the content foundation first. Start posting, build the library, and let the audience tell you what they'll pay for.

08
Visual
Identity
A compass for the visual work ahead. Direction for how the visual world should feel.
Mood
Warm and clean
Your visual mood should feel warm, approachable, and clean. Think natural light, simple plating, and colors that feel nourishing rather than overly styled. You want parents to look at your content and feel like this is doable, like they could make this food in their own kitchen today. Avoid anything too precious or Instagram-perfect. The food should look real, appealing, and easy.
Photography Style
Overhead and close-up shots with natural light
For recipe content, shoot overhead or at a slight angle to show the full plate or bowl, with close-ups of texture when it matters (like showing how thick a puree is or how a toddler can pick up a finger food). Use natural light whenever possible. Your backgrounds should be simple and neutral so the food is the focus. If you're filming talking-head content, shoot in a well-lit space with a clean, uncluttered background. The goal is clarity and warmth, nothing distracting.
Color Direction
Warm earth tones with soft greens
Warm terracotta, soft sage, golden ochre, cream, and charcoal: nourishing, grounded, and approachable for parents seeking doable nutrition.
Tone of Voice
Direct and practical
Your tone should be straightforward, warm, and confident. You're a parent who's done the research and knows what works, talking to other parents who want clear answers. You explain the why without overcomplicating it, and you give specific, actionable guidance every time. You do it from a place of wanting to help. Every post should leave someone feeling like they know exactly what to do next.
