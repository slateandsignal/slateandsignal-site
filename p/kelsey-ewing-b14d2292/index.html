Niche Directions
Direction 01
Hashimoto's navigation for the newly diagnosed
This is the most direct path. You help young women who just got diagnosed with Hashimoto's and don't know where to start. They're overwhelmed by conflicting advice, their doctor told them medication is the only option, and they're looking for someone who understands what it's actually like. You walk them through the functional medicine approach, one clear step at a time. Your content would be practical guides, myth-busting posts about what actually helps, and real talk about what the healing process looks like when you take it seriously.
Direction 02
Functional food as medicine for chronic issues
This widens the aperture slightly beyond Hashimoto's to include other chronic health issues that respond to food and lifestyle changes. You're the person who translates functional medicine into something approachable and actionable. Your content would focus on how food actually affects the body, what to try first, and how to know if it's working. This direction works if you want to serve a broader audience but still keep the functional medicine lens as your differentiator.
Direction 03
Building a health routine that doesn't burn you out
This angle speaks to women who've tried to fix their health issues before but couldn't sustain the changes because they were already exhausted. You understand burnout firsthand. You help people build routines that actually fit into their real lives. Your content would be about sustainable habits, what to prioritize when you don't have energy for everything, and how to make progress without adding more pressure.
Content Format
Talking-based content with a coaching funnel
Your instinct is to talk things through, and coaching is a talking-based service, so lean into that. Short video content where you're on camera, speaking directly to someone who's trying to figure out their health, works better for you than long-form written posts. You could do Instagram Reels or TikToks where you answer one specific question per video, bust a myth, or walk through a single practical step. The goal is to build trust so people want to work with you one on one. Pair this with a simple lead magnet like a free 20-minute clarity call or a guide to getting started with functional medicine for Hashimoto's, and funnel people into your coaching packages from there.
A 60-second video where you say something like, 'If your doctor told you food isn't medicine, here's what I wish I'd known when I heard the same thing,' then give one concrete example of a food-based change that actually moved the needle for you or a client. End with a call to action to book a free call. Post three of these a week and you'll start seeing traction.

06
Signal
Blind Spot
You're talking about what you do instead of what changes for the person who hires you

You're probably explaining your credentials, your process, or your philosophy, which are all fine, but they're not the reason someone books a call. People hire a coach when they see themselves in the transformation you're describing.

Your content needs to speak directly to the person who just got diagnosed, feels lost, and doesn't know where to start. Not women with Hashimoto's in general. The woman who's sitting in her car after a doctor's appointment, googling whether food can actually help, and finding your post. That level of specific.

You're also likely posting to a general audience instead of filtering for the exact person you want to work with. Every post should make someone with Hashimoto's think, 'She's talking to me.' If someone without that condition still finds it interesting, fine, but they're not the target. Specificity is what builds trust.

The fix is to shift every piece of content from explaining what you do to describing what changes when someone works with you. Instead of 'I help women with Hashimoto's through functional medicine coaching,' try 'You just got diagnosed and your doctor said there's nothing you can do except take medication. Here's what I did instead.' That's the difference between a service description and a message that actually lands.

07
First
Move
Post three times a week for 90 days, all aimed at one person

Your first move is to post consistently for 90 days, three times a week, every single post aimed at a woman who was just diagnosed with Hashimoto's and doesn't know what to do next. Not women with Hashimoto's in general. The newly diagnosed one who's overwhelmed and looking for a guide.

Each post should answer one specific question or bust one myth. Keep it short, under 90 seconds if it's video, under 300 words if it's written. End every post with a clear next step, either to book a free call with you or to download a simple guide. The goal is to build trust and funnel people into a conversation.

Track what gets the most engagement and which posts lead to DMs or bookings. After 90 days, you'll have enough data to know what's resonating and what's not. If you're still not signing clients after that, the issue is either the offer itself or the funnel.

Pair this with one simple lead magnet, like a free 20-minute clarity call or a PDF guide to the first 30 days after diagnosis. Make it easy for someone to raise their hand and start a conversation. You're not trying to build a massive audience. You need two clients a month. That's 24 conversations a year. If you post consistently and make it easy to book a call, you'll get there.

Visual
Identity
A compass for the visual work ahead. Direction for how the visual world should feel.
Mood
Soft, cozy, approachable
Your visual direction should feel like walking into a calm, well-lit bookstore or a cozy tea shop. Warm, inviting, simple, and uncluttered. You want people to feel like they can breathe when they land on your page. Think natural light, soft textures, muted tones, and plenty of white space. Nothing clinical or sterile. This is health coaching that feels human.
Photography Style
Natural, warm, personal
Photos should feel like they were taken in your actual life. Natural light, soft focus, warm tones. You on camera in a cozy sweater, a cup of tea on a wooden table, a notebook open next to a window. The vibe is approachable and real. If you're using stock photos, choose ones that feel like they could be from your own home or a quiet café.
Color Direction
Soft lilac, blush pink, warm cream
Soft lilac, blush pink, and warm peachy coral on a warm cream background with deep charcoal text: light, uplifting, and grounded.
Tone of Voice
Warm, clear, steady
Your tone should sound like a friend who's been through it and knows the way forward. Warm. Clear. Steady. You're helping them take the next right step. Write like you're talking to one person who just texted you asking for advice. Short sentences, plain language, and no jargon unless you're immediately translating it. You're a guide.
