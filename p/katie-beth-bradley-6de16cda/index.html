Signal
Blind Spot
You're documenting a journey without naming who it's for

Right now your content direction is the San Diego move, which is a strong narrative spine. The gap is that you haven't yet defined who specifically needs to watch you do this. Is it people planning their own cross-country move? People stuck in a city or situation they want to leave? People who need systems to manage a big life change? Those are three different audiences.

The risk is that you start posting and the content stays in documentation mode without ever connecting to a specific problem someone else is trying to solve. That's fine for the first month or two while you're building momentum, but eventually people need to know why they should care about your move beyond just liking you as a person.

The other version of this gap is that your operational strengths are clear, anticipating problems and simplifying systems, but you haven't yet decided whether those skills are the offer or just the method you use to document the journey. If systems thinking is the real product, the move becomes one case study among many. If the move itself is the product, the systems thinking is the lens you use to show it. Both are valid, you just need to pick one.

Start by posting the move content exactly as you described it. Pay attention to which posts get the most response and what questions people ask you. That will tell you whether your audience is showing up for the logistics, the emotional work, or the systems. Once you see that pattern, you can sharpen the positioning to match.

07
First
Move
Start posting the move prep now, before you leave

You said you want to build an audience first and then figure out the offer. That's the right instinct. The move to San Diego is happening at the end of the year, which means you have a few months to document the lead-up. Start now. Post about what you're doing to prepare, how you're thinking about the transition, what you're learning as you go.

Film short talking-head videos on your phone. Keep them under two minutes. Post them wherever feels most natural, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, all of the above. The platform matters less than the consistency. Aim for two or three posts a week. The goal is to build the habit and see who responds.

Don't wait until you have a polished plan or a clear offer. The content is the research. You're testing what resonates, what people ask you about, what feels natural to talk about. The audience will tell you what they need if you give them enough material to respond to.

Once you're in San Diego and settled, you'll have a body of content that documents the whole arc. That's when you can look back and decide what the real offer is. Maybe it's a guide for people planning their own move. Maybe it's a systems framework for managing big transitions. Maybe it's something else entirely. You won't know until you start.

Visual
Identity
A compass for the visual work ahead. Direction for how the visual world should feel.
Mood
Grounded, warm, alive
The visual mood should feel like someone standing on solid ground with sun on their face. Not overly polished or styled, just real and present. Think natural light, outdoor settings, textures that feel tactile. Sand, evergreen branches, warm wood, concrete. The mood is capable and warm at the same time. You're not performing ease, you're showing someone who's doing the work and enjoying it.
Photography Style
Natural, unposed, in motion
Photos should look like someone pulled out their phone and captured a real moment. You mid-conversation, packing a box, walking on the beach, sitting on the floor with a notebook. The style is documentary. Lighting can be imperfect. Backgrounds can be messy. What matters is that it feels like a real person's real life.
Color Direction
Sand, deep green, vibrant warmth
Sand, deep green, and vibrant terracotta create a palette that feels coastal, grounded, and alive: warm without being soft, natural without being muted.
Tone of Voice
Direct, unfiltered, grounded in logic
Your tone should sound like someone who's already thought three steps ahead and is telling you what they figured out. No fluff, no overly emotional language, no pretending things are harder than they are. You think in systems and you talk in straight lines. That doesn't mean cold, it means clear. You can be warm and direct at the same time. Use short sentences. Say what you mean. If something's obvious to you, say it's obvious. People will trust you because you're not trying to sound like anyone else.
