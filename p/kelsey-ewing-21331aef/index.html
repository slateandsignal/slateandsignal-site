06
Signal
Blind Spot
You're preparing more than you're putting work out there

Your instinct is to write or talk things out to yourself, then show them to your husband or close friends or family before sharing them publicly. That makes sense. You want to get it right. You don't want to make mistakes. But that same instinct is probably keeping you from posting consistently enough for people to find you.

You mentioned your dad's advice, if you're going to do something, do it right, and how you've reframed that as not being afraid to make mistakes. That reframe is good. Now you need to actually live it. The gap between what you want to build and where you are right now isn't about needing a better strategy or more clarity. You already know what you're doing and who you're helping. The gap is about volume and visibility.

You've been running this business for over a year without signing a client. It's because not enough people know you exist yet. Building a personal brand means showing up consistently in public. Every post you write and then don't share is a missed chance for someone who needs your help to find you.

The fix is simple but not easy. Start posting three times a week minimum, even if the posts feel imperfect. Talk about food as medicine, share what worked for you, teach one concept at a time. Let people see you doing the work before it's polished. The audience you're trying to reach doesn't need perfect content. They need someone who gets it and can help them. You're already that person. Now you just need to be that person in public.

07
First
Move
Post three times this week about food and Hashimoto's

Your first move is to create three posts this week, each one teaching a single concept about food as medicine for Hashimoto's. Don't overthink it. Pick three things you already know, things you've explained to friends or family before, and turn each one into a short post or video. One could be about why gluten matters for Hashimoto's. One could be about the best breakfast for thyroid health. One could be about how you learned to read your own labs.

Write them or film them the same day you come up with the idea. Don't sit on them for a week refining. Don't run them by five people first. Finish the post, post it, move on. The goal is momentum, getting used to putting work out there without needing it to be perfect. You already know this stuff. You're already qualified to teach it. The only thing stopping you from signing clients is that not enough people see you doing the work.

After you post all three, look at which one got the most response, comments, saves, messages. That tells you what resonates. Do more of that next week. Keep posting three times a week for the next month. Track how many discovery calls you book. If you're not booking any calls after a month of consistent posting, the issue is probably your call to action. Make sure every post ends with a clear next step, DM me if this sounds like you, or link in bio to book a free call.

This is not about building a massive audience before you start coaching. You can sign your first two clients with 200 followers if those 200 people are the right people and they see you showing up consistently as someone who knows how to help them. The work is to make yourself visible doing the thing you already know how to do. Start this week.

Identity

Mood
Soft, warm, approachable, cozy
Your visual mood should feel like walking into a bookstore or a good coffee shop. Warm, inviting, calm, a little bit feminine but grounded. You love light colors, lilac and pink especially, and you're drawn to minimalist, cozy spaces. The overall feel should be soft and approachable, the kind of aesthetic that makes someone feel safe and seen. Think natural light, simple compositions, a little bit of color to keep it from feeling sterile. You want people to feel like they can trust you and that you get what they're going through.
Photography Style
Bright, simple, a little bit of life
Your photography should be bright and clean, lots of natural light, simple backgrounds, nothing too busy or cluttered. Show real moments, your workspace, a cup of tea, a notebook, food that actually looks like food you'd eat. You're trying to show up as someone who knows how to help. Keep it simple and warm. A photo of your morning routine or a plate of food you made works better than a heavily styled flatlay. Let a little bit of your life show through without making the content about your life.
Color Direction
Soft lilac, warm pink, grounded cream
Soft lilac primary, warm blush pink secondary, grounded mauve accent, creamy background, charcoal text: feminine, calm, approachable, credible.
Tone of Voice
Warm, clear, like a friend who knows what they're talking about
Your tone should feel like talking to a friend who's been through it and knows how to help. You're warm and encouraging, you remember what people tell you, and you naturally cheer people on. Write the way you'd explain something to a close friend over coffee. Keep it clear and direct, no jargon unless you explain it right away. You're trying to sound like someone who gets it and can make this easier. Be specific, be kind, be honest about what worked and what didn't. Let people feel like you're on their side.
