06
Signal
Blind Spot
You haven't decided whether you're selling jewelry or teaching people to make it.

Your answers say you love making jewelry, you love talking about it, and you want to sell a product. That points toward making and selling finished pieces. But you also talk about crafting with friends, about problem-solving around physical limitations, and about education being a joy in your life. That points toward teaching.

Those are two different businesses. Selling finished jewelry means you make the thing, post it, sell it, and move on to the next piece. Teaching means you create content that shows people how to do what you do, and you monetize through courses, patterns, or memberships. You can do both eventually, but you can't build a clear brand until you pick one as the primary thing.

Right now your instinct to show finished work and sell it is stronger than your instinct to teach. You said you want to show the finished thing. You said you want to sell a product. That's a clear signal. If that's true, then your first move is to make jewelry, post it, and sell it directly. The teaching piece can come later if you want it to.

If you try to do both at once, you'll confuse your audience and dilute your message. Pick one. Build around it. Add the other later if it still makes sense.

07
First
Move
Make Ten Pieces and Post Them

Your first move is to make ten pieces of jewelry that you genuinely like, the kind of thing you'd wear yourself or give to a friend. Bright colors, ocean tones, happy yellow, whatever makes you smile. Finish them completely. Then take a short video of each one, hold it up, talk about it for 30 to 60 seconds, and post it.

You don't need a perfect shop set up yet. You don't need a website. You can sell directly through Instagram or Facebook messages if that's easier. The point is to prove to yourself that you can make something, post it, and sell it. Do that ten times. See what happens.

While you're doing that, pay attention to which pieces get the most response, which ones feel easiest to talk about, which ones sell fastest. That will tell you more about your direction than any amount of planning. You'll learn by doing.

Once you've posted and sold ten pieces, you'll know whether this feels good or not. If it does, keep going and start building the infrastructure around it. If it doesn't, then you know teaching or something else might be the better path. But you can't know until you actually do it.

Your visual mood is energetic and unpretentious. Bright colors, natural light, no fancy staging. The jewelry is the hero, you're holding it, the background doesn't matter. It should feel like someone showing you something they made because they're excited about it. Think cheerful, immediate, human.
Photography Style
Handheld and Honest
Your photography style is simple and direct. Hold the piece up, make sure the light is good enough to see the colors clearly, and talk. No tripod required, no elaborate setups. Phone camera is fine. The goal is to show the thing you made. If the video has good light and you can see the jewelry, it works.

There's warmth there, but also a no-BS quality. You're not here to perform, you're here to make things and talk about them.
