Niche Directions
Direction 01
Functional medicine for Hashimoto's in young women
This is the most direct path. You build content around what you're already coaching on: how young women with Hashimoto's can use food as medicine, what functional medicine offers that traditional medicine misses, and how to take your health into your own hands when your doctor dismisses you. Your own story of being told food isn't medicine becomes the bridge into this work. You'd post about thyroid health, anti-inflammatory eating, symptom patterns, and what it actually looks like to work with a functional medicine health coach. This is the clearest match to what you're building and who you want to attract.
Direction 02
Functional wellness for women who don't trust traditional medicine anymore
This is a slightly broader angle that still centers your core belief but opens up beyond Hashimoto's specifically. You'd talk about what happens when traditional medicine fails you, how to advocate for yourself in a broken system, and how functional medicine offers a different framework entirely. The audience here is women who've hit the same wall you did, they've been dismissed or told their symptoms are in their head, and they're looking for someone who gets it. This direction gives you more flexibility in what you post about while still attracting the Hashimoto's clients you want most.
Direction 03
Health coaching for women ready to leave burnout behind
This one leans into the burnout piece of your story. You left two toxic jobs, and you're building a business that gives you financial freedom and flexibility. You could speak to women who are burned out, chronically stressed, and dealing with the health consequences of that, Hashimoto's being one of them. The content would be about healing your body after years of pushing too hard, what chronic stress does to your thyroid and hormones, and how to rebuild both your health and your life at the same time. This angle attracts a slightly different audience but still feeds directly into your health coaching offer.
Content Format
Talking-head videos and written posts, both teaching-focused.
Your instinct is to talk things out, and you light up listening to other people speak, which tells me you'd do well with video content where you're teaching directly to camera. Short educational videos on Instagram or TikTok, longer teaching videos on YouTube, or even just voice-over-slide content if being on camera feels like too much at first. Pair that with written posts, either captions or standalone newsletter content, that let you go deeper on the functional medicine concepts you're teaching. You're not a process-show person, you're a teach-and-inspire person, so your content should reflect that. Monetization-wise, you'd use this content to build trust and authority, then move people into your coaching packages, and eventually into a course or membership once you have enough clients to know exactly what they need most.
A short video explaining why your doctor might dismiss food as medicine and what functional medicine does differently, paired with a caption that tells your own story of being told the same thing. Or a longer YouTube video walking through a sample anti-inflammatory meal plan for Hashimoto's, showing exactly what you'd eat in a day and why each choice matters.

06
Signal
Blind Spot
You're waiting for the content to feel perfect before you post it.

Your dad's old advice, if you're going to do something, do it right, is still sitting in the back of your head even though you consciously disagree with it now. You're stuck in the gap between knowing what you want to say and feeling like you've said it well enough to hit publish.

You're probably drafting things, showing them to your husband or close friends, revising them, and then either posting them inconsistently or not at all because they don't feel polished enough yet. That perfectionism is costing you momentum.

The truth is that your content doesn't need to be perfect, it needs to be consistent and clear. You already know more than your ideal client does about Hashimoto's and functional medicine. You're a natural listener and nurturer, which means you're probably great at explaining things in a way that feels approachable and supportive. That's already enough to start building trust. The polish can come later, after you've posted a hundred times and figured out what actually resonates.

Your first move is to make more of it. Post three times a week for the next three months, even if it feels messy or incomplete. Let yourself make mistakes in public. That's the only way you'll figure out what works and start attracting the clients you want.

07
First
Move
Post three teaching pieces a week for three months.

Your niche is clear, your offer is clear, and you already know what you want to teach. The only thing standing between you and your first client is volume. You need to show up consistently enough that people start to recognize you as the Hashimoto's and functional medicine person, and that only happens if you're posting multiple times a week, every week, for long enough that the algorithm and your audience both notice.

Pick a format that feels sustainable. If talking-head videos feel too hard right now, start with written posts or voice-over slides. If you love the idea of video but need a lower bar, record yourself talking to your phone like you're explaining something to a friend, no script, no editing, just hit record and post it. The format matters less than the consistency. You need to build the habit of posting before you worry about making it pretty.

Each post should teach one small thing. Why Hashimoto's flares happen, what anti-inflammatory eating actually means, how to read your thyroid labs, what to ask your doctor if they dismiss you, what working with a health coach looks like. That's it.

At the end of three months, you'll have posted around 36 to 40 pieces of content. That's enough for someone to scroll your feed and get a clear sense of what you do and who you help. That's when people start reaching out. You're not waiting for the perfect post to go viral, you're building a body of work that makes the right people want to work with you. Start now, even if it feels messy. The momentum will fix the rest.

Mood
Soft, cozy, and approachable.
Your aesthetic leans toward light, warm, and inviting spaces. Think bookstore corners with soft lighting, tea shops with mismatched mugs, and the kind of cozy minimalism that feels personal rather than sterile. Your visuals should feel like a calm, safe space where someone can exhale and trust that you're not going to overwhelm them. Light and airy, but never cold or clinical. Warm neutrals, soft textures, and just enough color to feel alive without being loud.
Photography Style
Natural light, soft focus, warm and lived-in.
Your photos should look like they were taken in your actual life. Natural light coming through a window, a cup of tea on a wooden table, a notebook open with a pen sitting on top of it, your hands holding a supplement bottle or a plate of food. Soft focus, warm tones, nothing too sharp or overly edited. The vibe is cozy and approachable, like you're inviting someone into your kitchen to talk about health over tea. You're not trying to look like a clinical expert, you're trying to look like a trusted friend who happens to know a lot about functional medicine.
Color Direction
Light, warm, and softly saturated.
A soft, warm palette anchored in peachy-pink and lavender, with a deeper rose accent and warm cream background: light, feminine, and inviting without losing maturity.
Tone of Voice
Warm, clear, and steady.
You're a natural listener and nurturer, and that should come through in how you write and speak. Your tone should feel like you're talking to a friend who just told you they're struggling with their health and you're sitting down to explain what's actually going on and what they can do about it. Warm and supportive, but never condescending or overly soft. You're not trying to be the loudest voice in the room, you're trying to be the steady, trustworthy one. Clear explanations, no jargon unless you're defining it, and always a sense that you're on their side. You believe food is medicine and traditional medicine often falls short, and that conviction should be present without being preachy. You're here to empower.
