06
Signal
Blind Spot
You are underselling how much your humor matters to your brand.

You said people come to you to laugh and spill their tea. That's not a side feature, that's the thing that makes your advocacy work. Without the humor, you're just another person talking about healthcare problems. With it, you're someone people actually want to follow.

Right now, you might be thinking the serious topics are the point and the humor is just how you happen to deliver them. That's backwards. The humor is what gives you permission to talk about hard things without exhausting your audience. It's the reason people will keep coming back even when the subject matter is heavy.

If you lean into the funny, sharp, truth-telling version of yourself, the advocacy will land harder because people trust you. They're not being lectured, they're being let in on something by someone who gets it and can still laugh about it.

The move here is to stop treating your humor as a personality quirk and start treating it as a strategic tool. Let it lead more often. When you're drafting a post about a healthcare frustration, ask yourself if there's a way to say it that makes someone laugh and feel seen at the same time. That's your voice.

07
First
Move
Post one healthcare truth-bomb this week.

Pick one specific thing about the healthcare system that frustrates you. Not a general complaint, a specific moment or pattern. Something like 'why do they schedule your appointment for 2pm and then make you wait in the lobby until 3:15' or 'why does every specialist act like they've never heard of your other conditions.'

Turn that into a short video or a quote post. If it's a video, look at the camera and say it the way you'd say it to a friend who just asked you about it. If it's a quote post, write it like a punchline. Make it sharp and funny enough that someone would send it to their group chat.

Post it and see what happens. You already know people respond to your voice because you hit 5k on TikTok. This is just doing that again, but more intentionally. You're not trying to build a whole content strategy in one post, you're testing whether this specific angle, healthcare truth-telling with humor, is the thing people want more of.

If it lands, do it again next week with a different frustration. If it doesn't land the way you thought, adjust. Maybe the humor needs to be sharper, maybe the topic needs to be more specific, maybe the format needs to change. That's the move.

Visual
Identity
A compass for the visual work ahead. Direction for how the visual world should feel.
Mood
Grounded and Approachable
Your visual mood should feel real and unpolished in a way that makes people trust you. Nothing overly styled or aspirational. Think candid shots, natural light, lived-in spaces. The kind of aesthetic where someone scrolling knows immediately that you're not performing, you're just showing up. Your content is about truth-telling, so the visuals need to match that energy. Warm, grounded, human.
Photography Style
Candid and Close
Your photography style should be close, direct, and unposed. Shots where you're looking at the camera like you're talking to one person. Natural light, simple backgrounds, nothing distracting. If you're showing your space, let it be messy in a real way. If you're showing yourself, let it be how you actually look that day. The point is to feel like someone's best friend texting them a video. This style supports your humor and your advocacy because it strips out any pretense.

Tone of Voice
Sharp, Funny, and Direct
Your tone should sound like someone who's seen enough to call it out and still laugh about it. You're not cynical, you're realistic. You say what other people are thinking but won't say out loud. Your writing should feel conversational, like you're texting a friend who just asked you a question. Short sentences. Punch lines. No fluff. When you're talking about something frustrating, let the frustration show, then land the joke. When you're giving advice, give it straight. No hedging, no 'just my opinion' disclaimers. People come to you because you're funny and because you tell the truth. Your tone should do both at the same time.
