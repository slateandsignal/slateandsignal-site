Interpretation
Guide
A quick guide to the words in this profile, so nothing here leaves you guessing what it actually means for you.
Signal
The pattern already running through your story, work, and instincts, whether or not you've named it yet. It's what people already sense about you before you say a word. Building a brand or a following works better when you build on a signal that's natural to you, instead of inventing one or chasing a trend that might be completely discordant with who you actually are. There shouldn't be any friction between you and what you're creating.
Archetype
The recognizable role your signal plays in other people's stories, the reason a stranger could describe what you do in one sentence to a friend. It shapes what you post, how you sound, and who ends up following you because they recognize the role you play.
Signal Strength
A 1 to 10 read of how clear and consistent your pattern is right now, based only on what you wrote. A lower number isn't a bad grade, it just means the pattern needs more specificity before it's ready to build content on. A higher number means you already have enough to start posting from today.
Suggested Direction
An actual recommendation. Where your answers point in terms of niche and what kind of content format genuinely fits how you communicate, so you're not staring at a blank page wondering where to start.

Three real categories your future posts can live in, pulled from what's already true about you rather than invented. Instead of watching a thousand other videos to see how everyone else does it, wondering where to start, what to talk about, and what people even want to see, you can pick a pillar from this profile and have a clear direction for what to create.

The thing your answers point to that you probably can't see from where you're standing. Everyone has one. It's usually the gap between what you already know and what you're actually showing people, and naming it is the fastest way to close it. This is the most direct section in your profile, on purpose. 06
Signal
Blind Spot
You're solving for everyone instead of someone specific.

When you say people come to you for everything and you could talk about anything in social media, that reads as capability. The problem is that people don't hire someone who can do everything. They hire someone who solves the specific thing they're stuck on right now.

Your instinct is to be helpful across the board. That's generous and it's probably why people like working with you. It's also what's keeping you from standing out in a crowded space. If you can help with anything, people don't know what to come to you for first.

The fix is to pick one transformation you create for people and build everything around that. Not the only thing you're capable of doing, just the one thing you're known for. You can still help with other stuff once someone's working with you. You just need a clear front door.

Look at the people you've already helped and find the pattern in what they needed most. That's probably your positioning. Build from there.

07
First
Move
Pick one person you've helped and tell that story.

Your first move is not to build an audience. Your first move is to document one real transformation you've already created for someone. Pick a friend or client whose results you're proud of. Write down what they were stuck on, what you noticed, what you told them to try, and what happened.

Turn that into a post or a video. Make it specific enough that someone in the same situation reads it and thinks, that's exactly where I am right now. That one piece of content will do more for your positioning than a month of general advice posts.

Once you've done that once, do it again with a different person. After three or four of these, you'll see the pattern in who you're actually best at helping and what problem you solve for them. That becomes your niche.

This is about proving to yourself and to other people that you can create a specific result. Start there.

08
Visual
Identity
A compass for the visual work ahead. Direction for how the visual world should feel.
Mood
Bright, clear and approachable
Your visual mood should feel open and energetic without being chaotic. Think clean backgrounds, good natural light, nothing cluttered or dark. You want people to feel like they're talking to someone who has their life together enough to help them figure theirs out. Avoid anything that feels corporate or overly polished. This should look like a real person who knows what they're doing.
Photography Style
Candid and unposed
Your photos should feel like someone caught you mid-thought. On-camera content where you're talking directly to the lens. Process shots of you working on Slate & Signal. Photos of your workspace or your screen with something you're building visible. Nothing stiff or staged. You want people to feel like they're getting the real version of you.
Color Direction
Icy blue, bright white and fuchsia accents
Crisp icy blue as primary, bright white background, vivid fuchsia accent tempered by use as highlight-only. Fresh, confident, open.
Tone of Voice
Direct, enthusiastic and real
Your tone should sound like you're talking to a friend about something you just figured out. Excited, clear, no jargon. You're not trying to sound like an expert, you're trying to help someone solve a problem. Use short sentences. Say what you mean. Don't hedge or soften things that should be said plainly. Your energy comes through when you're direct.
