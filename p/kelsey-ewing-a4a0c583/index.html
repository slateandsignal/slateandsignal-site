Niche Directions
Direction 01
Functional medicine for Hashimoto's
This is the most direct path. You help young women with Hashimoto's figure out what to eat, how to manage symptoms, and how to work with their bodies instead of against them. Your content would be educational but personal, built around the specific things you learned when you worked with a functional medicine coach yourself. The audience is narrow but motivated.
Direction 02
Taking your health into your own hands
This is a broader angle, less about Hashimoto's specifically and more about what it looks like to stop waiting for a doctor to fix you and start researching, experimenting, and advocating for yourself. You could talk about functional medicine as one tool in that toolkit, but the real hook is the mindset shift from passive patient to empowered researcher. This would appeal to women dealing with any chronic issue where conventional medicine hasn't helped.
Direction 03
Food as medicine for chronic health
This zooms out even further, focusing on the idea that what you eat directly affects how you feel, especially when you're dealing with something long-term like autoimmune issues, gut problems, or fatigue. You could build content around specific foods, meal ideas, what to try when symptoms flare, and how to think about food as a tool instead of just fuel. This angle would let you talk about Hashimoto's as an example without making it the only thing you cover.
Content Format
Written content with a personal voice, occasional talking
Your instinct is to write or talk things out to yourself first, then share them with people you trust. That suggests you'd do best with a format that lets you think through ideas before they go public, like a newsletter or long-form posts on Instagram or LinkedIn. You enjoy listening to inspirational people talk, which means your audience probably does too. You could start with written content that feels conversational and personal, then layer in occasional voice notes, Instagram Stories where you talk through an idea, or short videos where you share a specific tip. The key is to lead with your natural communication style, which is reflective and thoughtful, and add talking when it feels right.
A weekly newsletter where you share one thing you learned about functional medicine that week, a specific food or habit that helped you manage symptoms, or a story about a conversation you had with a client or friend. Each one ends with a small action the reader could try. Over time, this builds trust and positions you as someone who actually knows what works, which makes the coaching offer feel like the natural next step.

05
Content
Pillars
Three areas where your signal is strongest and most transferable to an audience.
Pillar 01
Food and symptoms
This pillar is where you talk about specific foods, how they affect the body, and what to try when you're dealing with a flare-up or chronic issue. It's educational but grounded in your own experience and the research you've done. The content here should feel immediately useful.
A post about three foods that helped you manage Hashimoto's symptoms when you were first diagnosed, written as a list with a short explanation of why each one works and how to add it to your routine.
Pillar 02
Navigating the health system
It's where you talk about finding the right practitioners, asking better questions, and trusting your own instincts about your body. This is the mindset side of the work.
A post about the moment your doctor told you food isn't medicine and how that conversation changed the way you approach your health. You could frame it as a turning point and talk about what you did next.
Pillar 03
Living well with chronic health
This pillar is where you share what it actually looks like to build a life around managing a chronic condition without letting it define everything you do. It's less about specific advice and more about the emotional and practical side of living well despite the constraints.
A post about planning a vacation when you have Hashimoto's, including what you pack, how you think about food while traveling, and how you balance enjoying yourself with taking care of your body.
06
Signal
Blind Spot
You are trying to build an audience and get clients at the same time, and it is not working.

You have been running this business for over a year without signing a client. It is a visibility problem. You are trying to sell coaching to people who do not know you yet, and no amount of posting or marketing will fix that until you build trust first.

It is just incomplete. Social media works when you are showing up consistently with content that makes people feel like they already know you and trust your perspective. You are probably posting sporadically, or posting things that feel too general, or trying to sell before you have built the foundation.

The other issue is that you are thinking about your business in terms of outcomes (two clients a month, financial freedom, brand deals) without a clear plan for how to get there. You are skipping steps.

What you need to do is build a small, engaged audience first. That means showing up consistently with content that is specific, helpful, and personal. Write about the things you actually know. Share what worked for you. Talk about the research you have done and the mistakes you made. Give people a reason to follow you that is not about selling them something. Once you have done that for a few months, the clients will come. But you cannot skip the trust-building part.

07
First
Move
Write one post a week for three months and do not try to sell anything.

Your first move is to commit to showing up consistently with content that builds trust. Pick one platform (Instagram, LinkedIn, or a newsletter) and write one post a week for the next three months. Each post should be about something specific you know or learned. Do not try to sell coaching. Do not ask people to book a call. Just share what you know in a way that feels helpful and personal.

Use the three content pillars to guide what you write. One week, write about a specific food or habit that helped you manage symptoms. The next week, write about navigating the health system or advocating for yourself. The week after that, write about what it is like to live well with a chronic condition. Rotate through those three categories and do not overthink it. The goal is to build a body of work that shows people you know what you are talking about and that you understand what they are going through.

At the end of three months, you will have twelve posts. That is enough for someone to scroll through your profile or archive and get a sense of who you are and what you care about. That is when you can start talking about coaching. But not before. Right now, you do not have enough content for someone to trust you yet.

The other part of this move is to stop waiting for permission or perfection. You mentioned that your dad used to say if you are going to do something, do it right, and you have learned to give yourself permission to make mistakes instead. Apply that here. Your first few posts will probably feel awkward or too general or not quite right. That is fine. Post them anyway. You will get better as you go, but only if you actually start.

Mood
Cozy, warm, and approachable
Your visual mood should feel like walking into a bookstore or a tea shop on a quiet afternoon. Soft, inviting, a little bit feminine without being overly precious. Think natural light, clean surfaces, and small moments of color that feel intentional but not loud. The aesthetic should make people feel like they can relax and trust you, like you are someone who has figured something out and is willing to share it without making it complicated.
Photography Style
Bright, uncluttered, and personal
Your photography should be simple and warm. Natural light, clean backgrounds, and a focus on the small details that make something feel real. A cup of tea on a table, a notebook open with a pen next to it, a bowl of food plated in a way that looks appealing but not overly styled. The goal is to make your content feel approachable and human, like someone could recreate what you are showing them without needing a full production setup.
Color Direction
Soft lilac and warm pink with neutral warmth
Soft lilac and warm pink with a deep plum anchor, set against warm cream and charcoal text: feminine, calm, and alive without being loud.
Tone of Voice
Warm, clear, and quietly confident
Your tone should sound like a friend who has been through something hard and figured out how to manage it, and now wants to help you do the same. It is warm, personal, and specific. You share what worked for you without claiming it will work for everyone. You ask good questions and listen well, even in writing. Your voice should feel like someone who knows what they are talking about but is not trying to prove it. The confidence comes from the specificity.
