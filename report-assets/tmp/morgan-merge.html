HOW TO READ
THIS REPORT

A few terms that will be helpful to understand as you read your report.

Signal

Your signal is what you naturally and unconsciously communicate to the world. The stronger it is, the more clearly your interests, instincts, and values work together to give your brand a solid foundation on which to build.

Archetype

In psychology and storytelling, archetypes are familiar human patterns. This is particularly useful to know as you build your brand because it helps make your brand feel consistent and gives your brand a recognizable role. It helps people understand what they can expect from you and gives your voice, content, and visual choices a consistent direction.

Constellation

The constellation in your report is a visual expression of your archetype. Each point of light represents a theme from your answers, brought together into a constellation unique to you. It tells the same story as your archetype.

Signal Strength

A 1-10 score for how consistent and specific your signal is right now, not how good or marketable you are. A lower score usually means the signal is real but not yet fully claimed out loud, not that it's weak.

The Niche Gap

Real research into what actual questions your audience is already asking. Especially the questions they’re not getting good answers to. Those gaps show you where there is room to say (or show) something useful and specific without repeating what everyone else is already saying. To stand out. An opportunity to become the authority within your niche.

Contrarian Belief

Something you believe, based on real experience, that goes against what most people in your niche currently say. These are listed because a contrarian belief stated clearly and often is one of the fastest ways an audience remembers who you are.

THE MARKET
OPPORTUNITY

Two groups of people are stuck on the same thing, and both of them are yours. Much of what follows comes from Reddit, a public website where people gather in open discussion groups by topic and talk to each other honestly, so you can read what teachers and parents say when no one is selling them anything. In a large discussion group for teachers, one teacher says outdoor education "seems broad and able to be interpreted many ways by many people," that it "seems hard to incorporate," and that "it is not state tested." In a similar group for Australian teachers, a teacher of fifteen years says the paperwork for taking students off campus got so heavy that the science, humanities and languages departments all cut back or stopped their trips. Parents come at it from the other side. In a parenting group, a mother of three who is trying to cut screen time asks "How can I create more of an interest in the outdoors for my kids? Animals, insects, flowers etc." In a group for mothers, parents who cannot afford summer camp describe filling entire weeks with the library and the park. Both groups have already decided they want this. What they are missing is one clear lesson they can actually run, tied to a real place and a real season, that costs nothing and was written by someone who has done it with real children.

Cost of the alternative: The money side of this is already in the open. A 2026 camp price guide from KidPlanr reports that brand name summer programs charge $400 to $700 a week, while city recreation departments, the YMCA and community nonprofits run full day programs for $100 to $250 a week, and some of those are free. Families who want this for their children are looking hard at that gap.

Unanswered demand: CommentShark, a service that studies YouTube comments, looked at 100,235 comments on education and how to videos. About 13 percent of them were genuine questions, and 77 percent of those questions never got a reply from the person who made the video. People are asking. Almost nobody is answering them.

The work itself is a library of outdoor science lessons built around real places, written so a teacher can get one approved and a parent can run one on a Saturday morning.

RECOMMENDED NICHE

No Budget Outdoor Science Lessons for K-8 Teachers

The teachers talking to each other on Reddit name two problems at once. They do not know exactly what outdoor education means, and the planning looks like more work than they can carry, so the idea stays on the shelf. In the Australian teachers group, whole departments are giving up on trips because of the approval paperwork, and the support that does exist sits at the institutional end, such as Garfield County Outdoors serving two districts with lesson planning support and the National Park Service Parks as Classrooms program tied to specific park sites. Your nineteen years, your garden program and your nature club let you supply the missing middle: a named lesson, held on school grounds or a nearby park, with the approval language written in advance.

What Already Exists

  • Homeschool Nature Study publishes an ultimate guide to nature study resources with year round support for homeschool families.
  • Run Wild My Child runs large roundups of outdoor activities and nature school content for outdoorsy parents, including 100-plus activity lists with printable checklists.
  • The National Park Service Parks as Classrooms program at Golden Gate builds inquiry based, standards aligned park programs, with applications closed for the 2026-2027 school year.
  • Teachers Pay Teachers lists teacher made printable resources, including free plant and science sets filtered by grade band.

The Gap You Can Fill

  • Teachers in the Reddit teaching group say the term outdoor education is unclear and the planning feels like a lot of work, so one named lesson with a time limit and a list of what to bring closes most of that distance.
  • Australian teachers describe approval paperwork pushing entire departments out of field trips, which makes a lesson that stays on school grounds, with the paperwork already written, worth a great deal.
  • In a gardening group, a special education teacher who was handed a garden club with no money and no gardening background asks for cheap projects that fit a club meeting once a month.
  • Free and low cost program guides such as the Treasure Coast roundup and the KidPlanr Bay Area guide list programs by county and price, leaving the question of what to observe once a family arrives entirely to the family.

REVENUE
POTENTIAL

Verified paths from lessons to income.

Paid Lesson Packs for Teachers

Printable, standards aware outdoor lesson sets sold directly to classroom teachers. Teacher buying runs on a personal card at small ticket sizes, documented at a $12 printable bundle and a $79 classroom app, so the model works through volume and repeat purchase across grade bands. Your nineteen years of middle school science and your garden program give you the two things buyers judge fastest: correct content and a management plan that holds thirty kids together outdoors.

Teachers buy printable lesson units with their own money on Teachers Pay Teachers, and they hear about what to buy from other teachers in Facebook groups for their grade level and in the Reddit teaching group. Source: Where to Find K-12 Teachers Customers, PainHuntr https://www.painhuntr.com/where-to-find-customers/education/k12-teachers, gathered September 14, 2026.

Local Screen Free Nature Day Programs

Half day and full day outdoor science sessions for families, priced into the community tier. Brand name programs post $400 to $700 per week while city recreation, YMCA and community nonprofit full day programs run $100 to $250 per week, and that lower band is where a one person program with a park and a field kit carries margin. Your nature club and your ability to settle a restless group in two minutes are the operating skills this tier runs on.

KidPlanr's 2026 Bay Area guide shows city recreation camps, YMCA branches and Boys & Girls Clubs running good full day programs for $100 to $250 a week. Source: Affordable Summer Camps Bay Area 2026, KidPlanr https://kidplanr.com/blog/affordable-summer-camps-bay-area-2026, gathered September 14, 2026.

Scholarship-Funded Summer Naturalist Weeks

Week-long outdoor science camps delivered through accredited channels with part of the fee covered by scholarship dollars. The American Camp Association reports fees ranging from less than $100 to more than $1,500 per week for accredited day and overnight camps, and ACA-accredited camps apply through the UltraCamp Foundation for Send a Child to Camp Fund support for families who need it. You can build a full week from your nature club material and run it at the lower fee tier with scholarship support closing the gap.

The American Camp Association lists accredited day and overnight camps through its Find a Camp directory, and it helps families pay through the Send a Child to Camp Fund. Source: How to Afford Camp, American Camp Association https://www.acacamps.org/parents-families/planning-camp/preparing-camp/how-afford-camp, gathered September 14, 2026.

District Outdoor Education Program Design

Contracted program design for schools: lesson planning support, equipment specification, teacher guidance and feedback loops for outdoor learning spaces and school gardens. Garfield County Outdoors fills this role across two named districts, Garfield Re-2 and Garfield 16, providing direction, resources, lesson planning support, equipment and feedback so teachers can run safe outdoor lessons. Your nineteen years inside a middle school and the garden program you led mean you can write the plan and then stand in front of the staff who have to deliver it.

Garfield County Outdoors supports teachers in the Garfield Re-2 and Garfield 16 districts, and Nancy Hauer, a 7th grade teacher at Rifle Middle School, describes what the field program taught her students. Source: Educator Resources, Garfield County Outdoors https://sites.google.com/garfield16.org/garfield-county-outdoors/for-educators/educator-resources, gathered September 14, 2026.
